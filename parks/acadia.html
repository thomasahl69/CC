Acadia

The Ancient World

Creation – AD 500Two years · Explore + Deep TrailMulti-age · family-style

Where the human story begins — creation, the first great civilizations, and the roots of faith, history, and ideas.

What your family studies in Acadia

Each park in Evergreen Homeschool is one era of history, studied for two years — first Explore, then Deep Trail — with Bible, science, literature, art, poetry, writing, geography, and nature study all woven into the same story. The whole family learns together while each child travels at their own path.

Year 1 · Explore Foundational stories

  1. In the Beginning — Creation & the first stories

    Genesis, the dawn of the world, and the earliest stories of God and people.

    For you, teacher

    Start with story, not facts. Read the account, wonder aloud together, and let the youngest simply listen and narrate back. You are laying a foundation of awe, not covering a checklist.

  2. Rivers of Civilization — Egypt & Mesopotamia

    The first cities, writing, and wonders — pyramids, pharaohs, and the cradle of history.

    For you, teacher

    Build a pyramid from blocks, write names in “hieroglyphs,” taste some dates. Hands beat handouts at every level; the goal is that the era feels real, not memorized.

  3. The People of Promise — The Old Testament story

    Abraham to the kings — the story of Israel woven right through the ancient world.

    For you, teacher

    Let the Bible be the spine everyone shares (that’s your Hearth time). Rangers can map the journeys; Wildflowers can act them out. Same story, four depths.

  4. Greece & Rome — Myths, ideas & empires

    Greek myths and the first democracies; the mighty, road-building Roman world.

    For you, teacher

    Read myths aloud like bedtime stories. If a myth raises a hard question, that’s a feature — pause and talk about it as a family rather than rushing on.

Year 2 · Deep Trail Deeper history & sources

  1. Reading the Sources — How we know the ancient world

    Primary sources, artifacts, and the detective work of history itself.

    For you, teacher

    This is Ranger-level fuel, but younger ones love “being a history detective” too. Give each child the same artifact photo and ask what they notice before you explain.

  2. Ancient Science — Astronomy, math & invention

    Early astronomy, geometry, engineering, and medicine — the ancients were clever.

    For you, teacher

    Do the experiment before the explanation. Watching a shadow move or a simple lever work teaches more than a paragraph, and it levels the table for every age.

  3. Classical Thought — Philosophers, law & rhetoric

    Greek philosophers and Roman law — ideas that still shape how we live and think.

    For you, teacher

    You don’t need a philosophy degree — just ask good questions and think out loud with them. Modeling “I wonder…” teaches more than having every answer.

  4. The Early Church — The life of Christ & Rome’s fall

    The Gospels, the early church, and the close of the ancient age.

    For you, teacher

    Let this be a gentle, worshipful close to the era. Narration, art, and prayer carry it better than a test — for you as much as for them.

Four paths — every child at their own level

WildflowerYoung learnersWonder, stories, songs, and hands-on play — no writing required yet.
MeadowDeveloping learnersNarration (telling it back), notebooking, maps and timelines, and simple research.
RangerOlder learnersPrimary sources, analysis, independent research, and real writing.
HearthThe whole familyBible, poetry, art, nature, and read-alouds everyone shares together.

The nine subjects, woven into this era

BibleGenesis through the Gospels and the birth of the church.
HistoryMesopotamia, Egypt, Israel, Greece, and Rome.
ScienceEarly astronomy, invention, and the ancients’ natural world.
GeographyThe Fertile Crescent, the Nile, and the Mediterranean world.
LiteratureMyths, Aesop’s fables, and ancient epics (by level).
PoetryThe Psalms and the rhythms of ancient verse.
ArtCave paintings, hieroglyphs, Greek pottery, Roman mosaics.
WritingFrom copywork and narration to essays on the sources.
Nature StudyThe plants, animals, and night skies the ancients watched.
