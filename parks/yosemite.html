The Medieval World

AD 500 – 1500Two years · Explore + Deep TrailMulti-age · family-style

Knights, cathedrals, and faraway kingdoms — the long, layered story of the Middle Ages across the whole world.

What your family studies in Yosemite

Each park in Evergreen Homeschool is one era of history, studied for two years — first Explore, then Deep Trail — with Bible, science, literature, art, poetry, writing, geography, and nature study all woven into the same story. The whole family learns together while each child travels at their own path.

Year 1 · Explore Foundational stories

  1. Kingdoms & Castles — Feudal life & knights

    Rome falls, castles rise — knights, lords, villages, and the shape of medieval life.

    For you, teacher

    Castles and knights sell themselves. Ride the natural fascination — build, draw, and act it out — and the “dry” parts (feudal roles, daily life) sneak in painlessly.

  2. The Spread of Faith — Monasteries & cathedrals

    The medieval church, patient monks, and the great cathedrals reaching for the sky.

    For you, teacher

    Copy an “illuminated” letter together, light a candle, keep it beautiful and quiet. Atmosphere teaches the medieval church better than dates do.

  3. Empires of the East — Byzantium, Islam, China & Japan

    A wider world — Byzantium, the Islamic golden age, and the great empires of Asia.

    For you, teacher

    This keeps the story from being Europe-only. A map and a few good picture books do the heavy lifting; you’re widening their world, not filling gaps.

  4. Plague & Turning — A world on the edge of change

    Trials, the plague, and a medieval world tipping toward a new age.

    For you, teacher

    Hard history handled gently. Name the sorrow honestly, keep it age-right for your Wildflowers, and let older ones sit with the weight — together.

Year 2 · Deep Trail Deeper history & sources

  1. Charters & Kings — Magna Carta & the roots of freedom

    Medieval law and the Magna Carta — the first seeds of the freedoms we know.

    For you, teacher

    Connect it forward: “this is where ‘no one is above the law’ began.” Rangers can compare it to today; the whole family can feel why it mattered.

  2. Medieval Learning — Universities, medicine & the stars

    The first universities, medieval medicine, astronomy, and clever invention.

    For you, teacher

    Try a medieval remedy or a simple astrolabe activity. Let them judge it as a scientist would — what worked, what didn’t, and how we found out.

  3. Cathedrals & Craft — Gothic art & the guilds

    Soaring Gothic cathedrals, illuminated manuscripts, stained glass, and the guilds.

    For you, teacher

    Make something — stained-glass with tissue paper, a guild “apprentice” project. Craft is the curriculum here, and it welcomes every age to the same table.

  4. Voices of the Age — Beowulf & Chaucer

    Reading the medieval world firsthand — its legends, heroes, and pilgrims.

    For you, teacher

    Read the exciting bits aloud dramatically. Rangers tackle the real text; younger ones get the retelling. Nobody is bored, nobody is lost.

Four paths — every child at their own level

WildflowerYoung learnersWonder, stories, songs, and hands-on play — no writing required yet.
MeadowDeveloping learnersNarration (telling it back), notebooking, maps and timelines, and simple research.
RangerOlder learnersPrimary sources, analysis, independent research, and real writing.
HearthThe whole familyBible, poetry, art, nature, and read-alouds everyone shares together.

BibleThe medieval church and Scripture faithfully preserved.
HistoryFeudal Europe, Byzantium, Islam, Asia, and the Mongols.
ScienceMedieval medicine, astronomy, and the roots of science.
GeographyThe Silk Road and the known — and unknown — world.
LiteratureKing Arthur, Beowulf, and Chaucer (by level).
PoetryBallads and the poetry of the age.
ArtIlluminated manuscripts, stained glass, Gothic cathedrals.
WritingNarration, copywork, and essays on medieval sources.
Nature StudyThe herb garden, the seasons, and the medieval land.
