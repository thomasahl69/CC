Renaissance & Exploration

1500 – 1700Two years · Explore + Deep TrailMulti-age · family-style

A world reborn and a world discovered — art, faith, and science awakening as explorers reach new horizons.

What your family studies in Yellowstone

Each park in Evergreen Homeschool is one era of history, studied for two years — first Explore, then Deep Trail — with Bible, science, literature, art, poetry, writing, geography, and nature study all woven into the same story. The whole family learns together while each child travels at their own path.

Year 1 · Explore Foundational stories

  1. A World Reborn — The Renaissance

    Rediscovery and dazzling new art — a burst of ideas, beauty, and confidence.

    For you, teacher

    Fill the room with the art. Prop up prints, try a “masterpiece” together, and let wonder lead. You’re growing taste and joy, not lecturing on movements.

  2. New Horizons — The age of exploration

    Explorers, ships, and the meeting of continents — the world’s map redrawn.

    For you, teacher

    Track voyages on a map with yarn and pins. Be honest that discovery had a cost; older ones can hold both the courage and the consequences.

  3. Faith Reformed — The Reformation

    A great turning in the church — Scripture in the language of the people.

    For you, teacher

    This is close to your heart, so share why it matters to you. Personal conviction, spoken plainly, disciples more than any worksheet.

  4. Science Awakens — The Scientific Revolution begins

    New questions and new tools begin to remake how we see the universe.

    For you, teacher

    Look up. A telescope or star app turns this era into an experience. Let a real observation spark the “how do we know?” conversation.

Year 2 · Deep Trail Deeper history & sources

  1. Masters & Masterpieces — Da Vinci & Michelangelo

    Renaissance genius up close — perspective, anatomy, engineering, and light.

    For you, teacher

    Do a real technique — one-point perspective, a fresco with plaster. Struggling with the craft themselves builds awe for the masters at every level.

  2. Explorers & Encounters — Firsthand accounts

    Primary accounts of exploration — and an honest look at its consequences.

    For you, teacher

    Read the actual journals. Ask “whose voice is missing here?” It’s a powerful, age-appropriate way to teach discernment with sources.

  3. Reformation Voices — Luther, Tyndale & the Word

    The words and people that put Scripture into the hands of ordinary families.

    For you, teacher

    Trace how your own Bible got to your table. Gratitude is the lesson; let it land as worship, not just history.

  4. The New Science — Galileo & Newton

    The birth of modern science — observation, evidence, and grand new laws.

    For you, teacher

    Roll balls, drop things, time them. The scientific method is best caught by doing it, and the youngest can predict-and-check right alongside the oldest.

Four paths — every child at their own level

WildflowerYoung learnersWonder, stories, songs, and hands-on play — no writing required yet.
MeadowDeveloping learnersNarration (telling it back), notebooking, maps and timelines, and simple research.
RangerOlder learnersPrimary sources, analysis, independent research, and real writing.
HearthThe whole familyBible, poetry, art, nature, and read-alouds everyone shares together.
