Revolutions & Pioneers

1700s – 1800sTwo years · Explore + Deep TrailMulti-age · family-style

Revolutions and pioneers — the birth of nations, world-changing inventions, and a rapidly remaking world.

What your family studies in Zion

Each park in Evergreen Homeschool is one era of history, studied for two years — first Explore, then Deep Trail — with Bible, science, literature, art, poetry, writing, geography, and nature study all woven into the same story. The whole family learns together while each child travels at their own path.

Year 1 · Explore Foundational stories

  1. Age of Revolutions — New nations are born

    The American and French revolutions — ideas of liberty reshaping the world.

    For you, teacher

    Big ideas, small hands: a “taxation” game at snack time makes revolution make sense to a six-year-old and a sixteen-year-old at once.

  2. A New Nation — Founding & the frontier

    The founding, the Constitution, and pioneers pressing west across the land.

    For you, teacher

    Pioneer life is pure hands-on gold — candles, journals, simple chores. Let them feel how hard and how brave ordinary days were.

  3. The Machine Age — Industry & invention

    Inventions, railroads, and factories remaking everyday life.

    For you, teacher

    Take apart something simple, follow how a thing is made. The wonder of “how does that work?” is the doorway into the whole era.

  4. A Divided Nation — Slavery & the Civil War

    The hard story of slavery, the Civil War, and the long fight for freedom.

    For you, teacher

    Handle this with honesty and gentleness. Match the depth to the child, tell the truth, and let it move you — modeling a tender conscience is the lesson.

Year 2 · Deep Trail Deeper history & sources

  1. Founding Documents — The words beneath the nation

    The Declaration and Constitution — and the ideas and debates behind them.

    For you, teacher

    Read the real words together. Rangers can debate them; everyone can hear how much rides on a single sentence.

  2. Inventors & Industry — The technology that remade life

    Electricity, the telegraph, machines — the tools that changed the everyday.

    For you, teacher

    Recreate a simple circuit or telegraph tap. A working spark makes the era unforgettable and levels the field for every age.

  3. Voices for Freedom — Abolition, firsthand

    Abolition and primary accounts — hard history told through real voices.

    For you, teacher

    Let the people speak for themselves through their own words. It builds empathy and discernment better than any summary you could give.

  4. Literature of the Age — Dickens & Austen

    The great 19th-century novels — social conscience, wit, and unforgettable characters.

    For you, teacher

    Read aloud in funny voices. Rangers read the full novels; younger ones get the abridged or the film after — same story around one table.

Four paths — every child at their own level

WildflowerYoung learnersWonder, stories, songs, and hands-on play — no writing required yet.
MeadowDeveloping learnersNarration (telling it back), notebooking, maps and timelines, and simple research.
RangerOlder learnersPrimary sources, analysis, independent research, and real writing.
HearthThe whole familyBible, poetry, art, nature, and read-alouds everyone shares together.
